# Product Requirements Document: Local Scriptorium v0.3

## Late Antique Thought Corpus, Auditable RAG Evaluation, and Blinded Review

Status: Ready for implementation planning

Target executor: 5.6 Luna Medium

Milestone: v0.3, additive to the released v0.2 methodology

## Problem Statement

Local Scriptorium v0.2 proves that a small, deterministic, local-first RAG evaluation harness can distinguish retrieval failures from answer-grounding failures. It is nevertheless structurally limited to one English translation of one work. Source paths, cleaning, chunking, questions, regression thresholds, and parts of the command-line workflow are Boethius-specific.

The current benchmark also maps questions directly to retrieval chunk IDs. That is sufficient for the original fixed corpus, but it becomes methodologically circular if chunking is later optimized against those same labels. The current question contract requires at least one relevant chunk, so it cannot represent plausible questions that the corpus should refuse to answer. It also cannot express that a cross-source or multi-hop question requires several distinct evidence groups.

The project therefore cannot become a credible multi-work portfolio project merely by downloading more books. It needs a versioned corpus pipeline, stable source-level evidence anchors, a richer benchmark contract, an auditable blinded review workflow, and an honest boundary between engineering judgment, product or client judgment, AI review, and external scholarly expertise.

## Solution

Local Scriptorium v0.3 will become a reproducible local RAG laboratory for Late Antique Platonism and its Christian reception. It will:

- preserve v0.2 as historical, runnable evidence;
- ingest a fixed, rights-reviewed corpus of nine public-domain English source units;
- normalize each edition deterministically while separating primary text from editorial material;
- create stable canonical passage anchors before producing retrieval chunks;
- compare structure-aware chunking and retrieval configurations using development data only;
- ship a 100-question benchmark with 70 development and 30 held-out questions;
- support answerable, unanswerable, single-passage, multi-passage, cross-author, and interpretation-sensitive cases;
- review benchmark artifacts through a project-local, blinded second-pass AI protocol;
- preserve original judgments, reviewer judgments, reconciliation, checksums, and staleness;
- evaluate retrieval and grounded generation separately;
- provide an optional local-model terminal experience with source citations and an interactive Unicode block-art medieval figure;
- document actual results, limitations, source rights, and review coverage without claiming scholarly validation.

The project-local classics protocol is the evaluator specification. A fresh model run or a human following that protocol is an evaluator instance. Same-model blinded review is procedural review, not independent scholarship.

## Implementation Decisions

- v0.3 is a new milestone. It does not retroactively change the meaning or measurements of v0.2.
- The public v0.3 corpus is titled Late Antique Platonism and Its Christian Reception.
- The active corpus contains nine source units: selected original Stephen MacKenna translations of Plotinus covering Enneads I–V; Thomas Taylor’s Iamblichus, On the Mysteries; E. B. Pusey’s Augustine, Confessions; Marcus Dods’s Augustine, City of God; Thomas Taylor’s Proclus, Elements of Theology; C. E. Rolt’s Pseudo-Dionysius, Divine Names; Rolt’s Pseudo-Dionysius, Mystical Theology; H. R. James’s Boethius, Consolation of Philosophy; and H. F. Stewart and E. K. Rand’s Boethius, Theological Tractates.
- Gregory of Nyssa, On the Soul and the Resurrection, remains blocked until the German or European rights status of the co-translation is resolved. It is not part of the default pack.
- Existing Plato and other candidate downloads are outside v0.3. They are neither deleted nor silently activated.
- User-owned modern translations are not public-corpus inputs merely because a physical or digital copy is owned.
- The deterministic offline path remains dependency-light and mandatory. Local generation and dense embeddings are additional profiles, not requirements for contract validation or CI.
- Source acquisition, normalization, canonical passage creation, chunking, indexing, and evaluation are separate deterministic stages.
- Every active source has a rights and provenance record. Status is approved, private-only, or blocked. Only approved sources enter the public pack.
- Primary text, translator notes, historical editorial material, modern website material, and distribution wrappers have distinct content classifications. Only explicitly approved classes enter the primary retrieval pool.
- Canonical passage anchors are stable edition-level evidence units. They are created before retrieval chunks and are the authority used by questions, answers, citations, and review.
- Retrieval chunks are derived artifacts tied to a chunker configuration and canonical passage range. Chunk IDs are not the permanent scholarly citation.
- Artifact schemas are versioned independently. The breaking question and evidence model is version 2 rather than a silent additive change to version 1.
- A version-2 question records answerability, primary task type, family or evidence-cluster identity, risk tags, acceptable evidence sets, required evidence groups, and split.
- Unanswerable questions may have no supporting evidence. Answerable questions must have at least one complete acceptable evidence set. Multi-hop questions must declare each required evidence group.
- Chunk-level relevance judgments are derived or reviewed for a particular chunker from canonical evidence. They are not stored as the only source of truth.
- The benchmark contains exactly 100 accepted questions: 70 development and 30 held-out.
- The primary task distribution is 30 single-passage textual questions, 20 within-work synthesis questions, 10 attribution or source-scope questions, 20 pairwise cross-author comparisons, 10 concept-tracing questions requiring at least three passages or sources, and 10 plausible unanswerable questions.
- Interpretation-sensitive and translation-sensitive are risk tags that may overlap the primary task types. Every such item receives review and may be excluded from scoring if it requires external adjudication.
- Split assignment is grouped by question family and evidence or concept cluster before stratification. Related paraphrases never cross splits.
- The held-out split is sealed after corpus, passage anchors, question rubric, reviewer protocol, and selected chunker are frozen. It is not used to choose chunk sizes, retrieval methods, k, fusion weights, prompts, or generation settings.
- The reviewer protocol supports chunk-quality, question-evidence, source-scope, and answer-claim reviews through separate rubrics.
- Reviewer packets omit expected answers, original judgments, rationale, retrieval method, ranks, scores, split identity, thresholds, benchmark results, and prior reviews.
- Standard source-consistency review is local-corpus-only. External references may be used only in an explicitly authorized external-adjudication mode and must be recorded.
- Review independence is reported as I1 for fresh, blinded procedural review using the same model family; I2 for fresh, blinded review using a different model or provider; and I3 for an independent human specialist.
- Review outputs are immutable second opinions. Reconciliation is a separate operation and canonical labels change only through a recorded adjudication.
- Review staleness follows a checksum dependency graph. Any changed dependency makes the affected review unavailable for release until refreshed.
- All 100 accepted questions receive blinded AI review. Every disagreement is human-adjudicated. At least 15 percent of AI agreements receive a source-balanced human spot check. Specialist-dependent unresolved items are excluded.
- Retrieval evaluation and answer-generation evaluation remain separate. Retrieval reports Recall@k, Precision@k, MRR, nDCG@k, hit rate, and complete-evidence success where applicable. Generation reports claim support, citation correctness and completeness, answer relevance, answerability, and refusal behavior.
- Unanswerable questions are not included in ordinary Recall@k denominators.
- v0.3 establishes new baselines and regression floors from development results. Generic thresholds copied from documentation are not release criteria.
- Keyword, BM25, TF-IDF, and reciprocal-rank hybrid remain deterministic baselines. A local dense embedding or reranker is admitted only as a separately identified experiment with recorded model metadata and measured value.
- The existing method name vector remains a documented v0.2 TF-IDF historical label. New v0.3 artifacts use unambiguous method identities.
- Retrieval and bootstrap work is restructured before full-corpus scaling so runtime does not grow through avoidable repeated sorting and resampling.
- The local answer experience is a first-class interactive command backed by a model adapter. CI uses a deterministic fake adapter; a real local-model run is recorded separately.
- The README uses a branded wordmark image; CLI output remains plain and machine-safe.
- README and portfolio claims are updated only after the corresponding reproducible artifacts exist.
- The correct description is blinded second-pass AI review or AI-assisted internal source-consistency review. Independent expert, peer-reviewed, gold-standard, and scholarly validated are prohibited unless a real qualified person performs the disclosed work.

## Testing Decisions

- Tests assert behavior at the highest stable seam: command results, artifact contracts, deterministic pipeline outputs, release decisions, and user-visible answers. Private helper implementation details are not the primary target.
- The existing v0.2 end-to-end flow remains a regression seam and must continue to ingest, evaluate, and report successfully.
- Source-register tests cover missing editions, pending rights, malformed checksums, disallowed paths, unapproved content types, and references to absent sources.
- Ingestion tests cover atomic failure, deterministic normalization, source-specific rules, wrapper removal, primary/editorial separation, and two identical-run checksums.
- Canonical-passage tests cover structural locators, stable identifiers, unrelated-source additions, content checksums, and traceability into the normalized edition.
- Chunking tests use representative prose, dialogue, verse, propositions, footnotes, and tractates. They verify boundaries, passage membership, source metadata, outlier reporting, and deterministic IDs.
- Question-contract tests cover answerable questions, empty-evidence unanswerable questions, alternative evidence sets, all-required multi-hop groups, unknown anchors, duplicate families, and invalid split states.
- Split tests prove that a family or evidence cluster cannot appear in both development and held-out data.
- Derived-relevance tests prove that passage-level truth maps predictably to a selected chunker and is regenerated when chunks change.
- Retrieval tests cover cross-author attribution, ties, empty queries, missing evidence, multi-hop completeness, stable ranking, method identity, and per-source slicing.
- Performance work is tested through deterministic synthetic fixtures and recorded timings rather than brittle machine-specific microsecond assertions.
- Review-packet tests prove that every forbidden control field is rejected and that randomization is seeded and reproducible.
- Reviewer-result tests cover every verdict, invalid evidence references, external-context disclosure, reviewer identity, protocol mismatch, and missing checksums.
- Reconciliation tests prove that the second-pass reviewer cannot mutate canonical data and that material disagreement remains unresolved without adjudication.
- Staleness tests change one dependency at a time and verify the exact affected reviews become stale while unrelated reviews remain current.
- Release-check tests cover missing review coverage, stale reviews, unresolved disagreements, blocked sources, unreviewed high-risk questions, and permitted exclusions.
- Generation tests use a deterministic fake model to verify context construction, canonical citations, refusal, uncertainty sections, and separation of textual evidence from interpretation.
- CLI tests invoke the process boundary and verify exit status, stderr errors, pure JSON stdout, redirected behavior, and explicit held-out execution.
- Reporting tests verify slices by author, work, question type, answerability, risk, and review status and prohibit comparisons between incompatible v0.2 and v0.3 datasets.
- Privacy and portability checks remain release requirements and expand to cover reviewer packets, source registers, and local model metadata.
- The held-out benchmark is executed only as a release-candidate gate. Development tests use development fixtures.

## Out of Scope

- Claiming that an AI evaluator is a person, classicist, independent scholar, or peer reviewer.
- Original-language Greek or Latin philology.
- Resolving contested authorship, chronology, doctrine, or translation questions without an external specialist.
- Ingesting copyrighted modern books or translations without an applicable license or permission.
- A general OCR platform for arbitrary scans.
- A universal classical-philosophy corpus beyond the approved Late Antique pack.
- Cloud deployment, authentication, multi-tenancy, mobile applications, or a graphical user interface.
- Fine-tuning an embedding model or language model.
- Requiring a cloud API for CI or the default evaluation path.
- Automatically publishing to Jira, GitHub Issues, or another external tracker.
- Production service-level objectives, alerting, or distributed vector infrastructure.
- Replacing v0.2 data, reports, thresholds, or historical claims.
- Shipping Gregory of Nyssa while its selected translation remains rights-pending.
- Adding a reranker, HyDE, query expansion, or another sophisticated retrieval feature without a development-set experiment demonstrating a need.
- Pushing, tagging, releasing, or changing public repository settings as part of implementation.
